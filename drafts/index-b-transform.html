images · signals · the transforms between them

Every imaging system convolves, whether you asked it to or not: a lens, a sensor, a hand that moved. The Fourier transform is nothing more mysterious than the eigenbasis of that operator: change coordinates and the smearing turns into a multiplication.

That is the move I keep coming back to, and it is much bigger than Fourier. Wavelets when the interesting part is local, Radon when the evidence arrives as projections, Hough when a shape is easier to count than to trace. Pick the basis in which the problem is diagonal, and the hard part stops being the arithmetic and becomes the choosing. What I write about is that choice: which transform, at what cost, and what it quietly assumes about the data on the way in.

Then there is the code, which is Rust now and was C++ before. I want the type of a value to carry what the value means: the grid, the units, the curve it has already been through. Then a mismatch is a compile error rather than an image that looks a bit off.
