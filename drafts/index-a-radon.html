images · signals · the transforms between them

I like the transforms that carry a picture out of the domain it was measured in and into one where the thing you were looking for is finally obvious.

A straight line in an image is hard to find. A point in Hough space is not. A blur is a hard operation to undo. A division in the Fourier domain is not. Fourier, wavelet, Radon, Hough: each is a change of basis that turns a hard question into an easy one, then hands the answer back to where it came from. Most of what I write about lives in that round trip: what the transform buys you, what it costs, and where the resolution limit sits that decides whether the answer was ever in the data at all.

The other half is making it hold up in code. Rust now, C++ before it. I want the type of a value to say what the value means: the grid it was sampled on, the light it stands for, the curve it has already been through. Then the compiler objects on the line where the mistake happens.
