My doctorate was on restoring underwater images — modelling what happens to light on its way from a scene to a sensor when the medium in between refuses to cooperate. Fifteen years later I am still doing the same thing with better tools.

Since 2016 I have been a software architect, and since 2020 a team lead, writing C++/Qt for molecular beam epitaxy systems: machines where a wrong number does not throw an exception, it grows the wrong crystal.

Almost every expensive bug I have chased was never really a bug. A channel order nobody wrote down. A gamma curve everyone assumed. A pyramid level that drifted half a pixel. I want that class of mistake to be unrepresentable — the meaning of a number encoded in its type, so the compiler objects on the line where it happens instead of a reviewer noticing three months later.
