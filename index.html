A series

Type-Safe Computer Vision

Three classic computer vision bugs that have each cost the field twenty years. None of them is a logic bug. Every one of them is a type bug — correct functions exchanging values whose meaning was never written down.

An image, in almost every vision library, is an array of numbers. Whether those numbers are red-first or blue-first, gamma-encoded or linear, at pyramid level 2 or level 3 — that lives in documentation, in variable names, in your memory. Everywhere except in the data. So the compiler, the one tool that checks your assumptions on every line, has been told nothing, and checks nothing.

The fix is not more care. Care doesn't scale.

Each post takes one bug, shows it small enough to be undeniable, then shows the same bug where no human is looking — inside a tensor, inside a production pipeline. The payoff is always a compile error.

    The Blue Face Bug

    Your camera says blue-green-red. Your model says red-green-blue. The bytes in between say nothing at all.

    128 Is Not the Middle

    Average a black pixel and a white pixel. You wrote (0 + 255) / 2 and got 128 — and that answer is wrong by a margin you can see from across the room.

    Beginner–intermediate · gamma and interpolation, Srgb8, SrgbGamma

  3. Part 3

    Climbing the Pyramid

    Sizes that stop matching, two “scales” that aren’t the same thing, and coordinates that quietly drift half a pixel per step.

    Intermediate · scale space, pyramids, half-pixel drift
